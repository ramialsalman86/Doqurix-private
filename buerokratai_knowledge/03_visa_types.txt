GERMAN VISA TYPES FOR NON-EU CITIZENS
======================================
Source: Federal Foreign Office (Auswärtiges Amt), BAMF
Last Updated: January 2026

OVERVIEW OF VISA CATEGORIES
---------------------------
Germany distinguishes between:
1. Schengen Visa (short-stay, up to 90 days)
2. National Visa (long-stay, more than 90 days)
3. Residence Permits (issued in Germany)

SCHENGEN VISA (TYPE C)
----------------------
Duration: Up to 90 days within 180-day period
Purpose: Tourism, business visits, family visits, conferences

Requirements:
- Valid passport (at least 3 months beyond stay, issued within last 10 years)
- Completed application form
- Biometric photos
- Travel insurance (minimum €30,000 coverage)
- Proof of accommodation
- Proof of financial means (€45-100 per day)
- Flight itinerary
- Purpose documentation

Fee: €80 (adults), €40 (children 6-12), free (children under 6)
Processing: 15 calendar days (can extend to 45 days)

Visa-free countries: USA, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, UK, etc.
- Can enter for 90 days without visa
- Cannot work without work permit

NATIONAL VISA (TYPE D)
----------------------
For stays longer than 90 days. Must apply BEFORE entering Germany.

Types of National Visas:

1. EMPLOYMENT VISA (Visum zur Beschäftigung)
   Requirements:
   - Job offer from German employer
   - Qualification recognition (if applicable)
   - Federal Employment Agency approval (in most cases)
   - Health insurance
   Fee: €75

2. EU BLUE CARD VISA
   Requirements:
   - University degree (recognized)
   - Job offer with salary above threshold
   - See separate Blue Card knowledge file
   Fee: €75

3. JOB SEEKER VISA (§ 20 AufenthG)
   Purpose: 6 months to search for qualified employment
   Requirements:
   - University degree (recognized in Germany)
   - Proof of financial means (~€11,000 or blocked account)
   - Health insurance
   - Accommodation proof
   - CV and motivation letter
   Validity: 6 months (non-extendable)
   Cannot work: Only job searching allowed
   Fee: €75

4. STUDENT VISA (§ 16b AufenthG)
   Purpose: Study at German university
   Requirements:
   - University admission letter (Zulassungsbescheid)
   - Proof of financing (€11,208/year in blocked account - 2024 rate)
   - Health insurance
   - Language proficiency (depends on program)
   - Secondary school leaving certificate
   Validity: 1-2 years, renewable
   Work allowed: 120 full days or 240 half days per year
   Fee: €75

5. LANGUAGE COURSE VISA (§ 16f AufenthG)
   Purpose: Intensive German language course
   Requirements:
   - Course enrollment (minimum 18 hours/week)
   - Financing proof
   - Health insurance
   Duration: Up to 12 months
   Cannot work: No employment allowed
   Fee: €75

6. FAMILY REUNIFICATION VISA
   
   Spouse Visa (§ 28, 30 AufenthG):
   Requirements:
   - Valid marriage
   - Spouse in Germany has valid residence permit
   - Basic German (A1) - exceptions apply
   - Adequate living space
   - Financial security
   
   Child Visa (§ 32 AufenthG):
   Requirements:
   - Child under 18
   - Both parents or parent with custody in Germany
   - Adequate living space

7. FREELANCE/SELF-EMPLOYMENT VISA (§ 21 AufenthG)
   Requirements:
   - Business plan
   - Financing proof
   - Evidence of economic interest or regional need
   - Professional experience and qualifications
   - Health insurance
   Fee: €75

8. VOLUNTEER VISA (§ 19e AufenthG)
   Purpose: Voluntary service programs (FSJ, BFD, etc.)
   Requirements:
   - Agreement with recognized volunteer organization
   - Age typically 18-26
   - Pocket money provided by organization
   Fee: €75

RESIDENCE PERMITS (AUFENTHALTSTITEL)
------------------------------------
Issued in Germany by Ausländerbehörde, not embassies

Types:
1. Aufenthaltserlaubnis (Temporary Residence Permit)
   - Purpose-bound (work, study, family)
   - Usually 1-3 years, renewable

2. Blaue Karte EU (EU Blue Card)
   - For highly qualified workers
   - Up to 4 years

3. Niederlassungserlaubnis (Permanent Residence)
   - Unlimited duration
   - After 5 years with residence permit (or faster with Blue Card)
   - Requirements:
     * 60 months pension contributions
     * Adequate German (B1)
     * Adequate living space
     * No criminal record
     * Basic knowledge of German law and society

4. Erlaubnis zum Daueraufenthalt-EU (EU Long-term Residence)
   - Similar to Niederlassungserlaubnis
   - Additional EU mobility rights
   - After 5 years legal residence

SPECIAL CATEGORIES
------------------

Skilled Worker Visa (Fachkräfteeinwanderungsgesetz):
Since March 2020, expanded since November 2023
- Vocational qualifications now accepted (not just university)
- Recognition of foreign qualifications required
- Experience-based immigration possible in certain cases

Opportunity Card (Chancenkarte) - Since June 2024:
Points-based system for job seekers without job offer
Points for:
- Qualification recognition
- German language (A1/A2/B1/B2)
- English language (B2/C1)
- Professional experience
- Age (under 35)
- Previous Germany stay
Minimum: 6 points required
Validity: 1 year
Work allowed: 20 hours/week in any job while searching

VISA APPLICATION TIMELINE
-------------------------
Recommended timeline before planned arrival:
- 3-6 months: Gather documents
- 3 months: Schedule visa appointment at embassy
- 2 months: Submit application
- 1-2 months: Processing
- 2 weeks: Receive visa

Peak times (longer processing):
- July-September
- Before university semesters
