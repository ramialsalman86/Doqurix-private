GERMAN HEALTH INSURANCE FOR IMMIGRANTS
=======================================
Source: GKV-Spitzenverband, BMG (Federal Ministry of Health)
Last Updated: January 2026

OVERVIEW
--------
Health insurance is MANDATORY in Germany (since 2009).
All residents must have health insurance - no exceptions.
This applies to everyone: employees, self-employed, students, and retirees.

TWO TYPES OF HEALTH INSURANCE
-----------------------------

1. STATUTORY HEALTH INSURANCE (GKV - Gesetzliche Krankenversicherung)
   Public/state system, non-profit, ~90% of population

2. PRIVATE HEALTH INSURANCE (PKV - Private Krankenversicherung)
   Private companies, for-profit, ~10% of population

STATUTORY HEALTH INSURANCE (GKV)
--------------------------------

Who MUST have GKV:
- Employees earning up to €69,300/year (2024 threshold)
- Students at German universities
- Unemployed receiving Arbeitslosengeld
- Pensioners who were in GKV most of their working life

Major GKV providers:
- TK (Techniker Krankenkasse) - largest, good for English speakers
- AOK (various regional) - widespread, traditional
- BARMER - second largest
- DAK-Gesundheit
- IKK - for craftsmen originally
- BKK (various company funds)
- HEK
- KKH

All GKV providers offer SAME basic coverage (legally mandated)
Differences: Additional services, customer service, bonus programs

GKV CONTRIBUTION RATES 2024/2025
--------------------------------
Base contribution: 14.6% of gross salary
Additional contribution: ~1.3-2.5% (varies by provider)
Total: ~15.9-17.1% of gross salary

Split: Employee pays ~half, employer pays ~half
Employee contribution: ~7.95-8.55% of gross salary

Monthly contribution ceiling:
- Maximum contribution based on €5,175/month gross (2024)
- Maximum employee share: ~€450/month

GKV COVERAGE INCLUDES
---------------------
✓ Doctor visits (GP and specialists)
✓ Hospital treatment
✓ Prescription medications (with €5-10 co-pay)
✓ Dental checkups (basic treatment)
✓ Maternity care (100% covered)
✓ Mental health treatment
✓ Rehabilitation
✓ Preventive care
✓ Sick leave pay (Krankengeld) after 6 weeks
✓ Vaccinations
✓ Medical aids (glasses, hearing aids - partial)

GKV DOES NOT COVER
------------------
✗ Single/private hospital rooms
✗ Chief physician treatment
✗ Some dental procedures (crowns, implants - partial coverage)
✗ Alternative medicine (usually)
✗ Cosmetic procedures
✗ Glasses/contacts (only partially for children)

PRIVATE HEALTH INSURANCE (PKV)
------------------------------

Who CAN choose PKV:
- Employees earning above €69,300/year (Versicherungspflichtgrenze)
- Self-employed and freelancers (any income)
- Civil servants (Beamte)
- Some students

Who CANNOT join PKV:
- Employees earning below €69,300/year
- Unemployed receiving Arbeitslosengeld
- Most retirees (if in GKV before)

IMPORTANT: Switching from PKV back to GKV is very difficult after age 55!

PKV CONTRIBUTION RATES
----------------------
Based on:
- Age at entry
- Health status (medical questionnaire)
- Chosen coverage level
- Deductibles

Typical costs:
- Young, healthy person: €200-400/month
- Middle-aged: €400-700/month
- Elderly: €600-1000+/month

PKV ADVANTAGES
--------------
✓ Often faster appointments
✓ Chief physician treatment
✓ Private hospital rooms
✓ Better dental coverage
✓ More alternative medicine
✓ Customizable coverage
✓ Lower premiums when young

PKV DISADVANTAGES
-----------------
✗ Premiums increase with age
✗ No income-based pricing
✗ Family members not free (each needs own policy)
✗ Difficult to return to GKV
✗ Must pay upfront, get reimbursed
✗ Pre-existing conditions can mean exclusions or higher premiums

HEALTH INSURANCE FOR SPECIFIC GROUPS
------------------------------------

1. EMPLOYEES
   - Automatically enrolled in GKV through employer
   - Can opt for PKV if earning above threshold
   - Employer handles registration and contributions

2. STUDENTS
   - Student GKV rate: ~€120/month (including nursing care)
   - Must be under 30 years old
   - Must be enrolled at recognized university
   - Over 30: Can get voluntary GKV or private
   - EU students: EHIC may work, but limited

3. BLUE CARD HOLDERS
   - Must have health insurance for application
   - GKV or PKV acceptable
   - Family covered under GKV

4. SELF-EMPLOYED/FREELANCERS
   - Can choose GKV (voluntary) or PKV
   - GKV voluntary: Minimum ~€200/month
   - No employer contribution - pay full amount yourself

5. JOB SEEKERS (with visa)
   - Must prove health insurance for visa
   - Travel insurance NOT sufficient for long-term visa
   - Need incoming insurance or private health insurance

EHIC - EUROPEAN HEALTH INSURANCE CARD
-------------------------------------
For EU/EEA/Swiss citizens:
- Valid for temporary stays
- NOT valid for moving to Germany permanently
- NOT valid for planned medical treatment
- Must get German insurance when becoming resident

TRAVEL/INCOMING INSURANCE
-------------------------
For visa applications:
- Minimum coverage: €30,000
- Valid for Schengen visa applications
- NOT valid for residence permits
- NOT valid for Blue Card

Popular incoming insurance:
- Mawista
- Care Concept
- HanseMerkur
- Usually €30-100/month

HOW TO GET HEALTH INSURANCE
---------------------------

For employed workers:
1. Employer provides registration form
2. Choose GKV provider
3. Employer handles paperwork
4. Insurance starts from day 1 of employment

For students:
1. Apply at GKV provider (TK, AOK, etc.)
2. Need: Enrollment certificate, passport, Anmeldung
3. Get Versicherungsbescheinigung for university
4. Takes 1-2 weeks

For freelancers/self-employed:
1. Contact GKV provider directly
2. Or choose PKV provider
3. Provide: Business registration, tax number, passport

IMPORTANT DEADLINES
-------------------
- Must have insurance from day 1 in Germany
- Retroactive coverage: If you register late, you may owe back premiums
- Employment: Automatically covered from start date
- Students: Must prove insurance for enrollment
