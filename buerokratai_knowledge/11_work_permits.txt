WORK PERMITS AND EMPLOYMENT IN GERMANY (ARBEITSERLAUBNIS)
=========================================================
Source: Federal Employment Agency (Bundesagentur für Arbeit), BAMF
Last Updated: January 2026

OVERVIEW
--------
Work permit rules depend on nationality and visa type.
EU/EEA citizens have automatic work rights.
Non-EU citizens need residence permit with work authorization.

EU/EEA/SWISS CITIZENS
---------------------
Countries: All 27 EU countries + Iceland, Liechtenstein, Norway, Switzerland

Work Rights:
✓ NO work permit needed
✓ Full freedom to work in Germany
✓ Can start working immediately
✓ Same rights as German citizens
✓ No employer restrictions

Only need:
- Valid national ID or passport
- Register residence (Anmeldung) within 2 weeks
- Apply for EU Freizügigkeitsbescheinigung (optional)

NON-EU CITIZENS - GENERAL RULES
-------------------------------
Must have residence permit (Aufenthaltstitel) that allows work.
Check your permit - it should state:
"Erwerbstätigkeit gestattet" (Employment permitted) or
"Beschäftigung gestattet" (Work permitted)

If permit says:
"Erwerbstätigkeit nicht gestattet" - NO work allowed
"Beschäftigung nur mit Genehmigung" - Work only with additional permit

TYPES OF WORK-RELATED RESIDENCE PERMITS
---------------------------------------

1. EU BLUE CARD (Blaue Karte EU)
   For highly qualified workers
   Requirements:
   - University degree (recognized)
   - Job offer with minimum salary
   - Salary 2025: €45,300/year (standard)
   - Salary 2025: €41,041.80/year (shortage occupations)
   
   Shortage occupations include:
   - IT and tech professionals
   - Engineers
   - Doctors
   - Scientists
   - Mathematicians
   
   Benefits:
   - Fast track to permanent residence (21-33 months)
   - Family can work immediately
   - Can change employers easily
   - Valid across EU after 18 months

2. SKILLED WORKER VISA (Fachkräfteeinwanderung)
   For qualified professionals
   
   a) Academic professionals (§18b AufenthG)
      - Recognized university degree
      - Related job offer
      - No minimum salary for non-Blue Card
   
   b) Vocational training (§18a AufenthG)
      - Recognized vocational qualification
      - Related job offer
      - 2+ years training equivalent

3. OPPORTUNITY CARD (Chancenkarte) - NEW since June 2024
   Points-based system for job seekers
   
   Points for:
   - Qualifications
   - Language skills (German/English)
   - Age (under 35 preferred)
   - Work experience
   - Germany connection
   
   Minimum: 6 points needed
   Duration: 12 months to find work
   Can work 20 hours/week while searching

4. JOB SEEKER VISA (§20 AufenthG)
   For university graduates
   - 6 months to find qualified employment
   - Cannot work during job search (except trial work)
   - Must prove financial means

5. INTRA-COMPANY TRANSFER (ICT Card)
   For employees transferred within same company
   - 3+ years employment with company
   - Manager, specialist, or trainee position
   - Valid up to 3 years

6. SELF-EMPLOYMENT VISA (§21 AufenthG)
   For freelancers and entrepreneurs
   Requirements:
   - Business plan
   - Proof of financing
   - Economic interest for Germany
   - Viable business concept

7. RESEARCHER VISA (§18d AufenthG)
   For scientific researchers
   - Research agreement with German institution
   - Doctorate or suitable qualification
   - Can teach alongside research

RECOGNITION OF QUALIFICATIONS
-----------------------------

University Degrees:
- Check recognition: anabin.kmk.org
- Categories: H+ (comparable), H+/- (partly), H- (not comparable)
- If not H+: May need individual assessment

Vocational Qualifications:
- Recognition database: anerkennung-in-deutschland.de
- Process can take 3-6 months
- May require additional qualification

Regulated Professions:
Medical doctors, lawyers, teachers, engineers (in some states)
MUST have recognition before practicing

Recognition costs: €100-600 depending on profession

EMPLOYER OBLIGATIONS
--------------------

Employers must:
1. Check employee's work permit status
2. Keep copy of residence permit
3. Report to foreigner's office when contract ends
4. Cannot employ people without work authorization
   (Fine: Up to €500,000!)

BUNDESAGENTUR FÜR ARBEIT APPROVAL
---------------------------------
Some work permits require BA approval.
BA checks:
- Labor market conditions
- Comparable working conditions
- Appropriate salary

Positions NOT requiring BA approval:
- EU Blue Card holders
- University graduates in qualified positions
- ICT Card holders
- Researchers
- Certain shortage occupations

MINIMUM WAGE (MINDESTLOHN) 2025
-------------------------------
General minimum wage: €12.82/hour
Applies to almost all workers regardless of nationality

Exceptions:
- Internships (certain types)
- Long-term unemployed (first 6 months)
- Some apprentices

WORKING HOURS
-------------
Working Time Act (Arbeitszeitgesetz):
- Maximum: 8 hours/day (can be 10 with compensation)
- Maximum: 48 hours/week average
- Rest periods: 11 hours between shifts
- Breaks: 30 min after 6 hours, 45 min after 9 hours

EMPLOYMENT CONTRACT (ARBEITSVERTRAG)
------------------------------------
German law requires written contract for:
- Job description
- Salary
- Working hours
- Holiday entitlement
- Notice periods
- Probationary period terms

Minimum by law:
- 20 vacation days (5-day week)
- 24 vacation days (6-day week)
- Many employers offer 25-30 days

Notice periods (statutory minimums):
- During Probezeit (trial): 2 weeks
- After trial: 4 weeks to 15th or end of month
- Increases with tenure

TRIAL PERIOD (PROBEZEIT)
------------------------
- Maximum: 6 months
- Shorter notice period: 2 weeks
- Cannot extend beyond 6 months
- No reason needed for termination during Probezeit

MINI-JOB AND MIDI-JOB
---------------------

Mini-Job (Geringfügige Beschäftigung):
- Maximum €538/month (2024)
- Employer pays flat-rate social security
- Employee: No social contributions (can opt-in for pension)
- Limited work rights for some visa holders!

Midi-Job (Übergangsbereich):
- €538.01 - €2,000/month
- Reduced employee contributions
- Full social security coverage

FREELANCE WORK (SELBSTSTÄNDIGKEIT)
----------------------------------

Freiberufler (Freelancer):
Certain professions recognized as "free" professions:
- Writers, artists, translators
- IT consultants (often)
- Doctors, lawyers, architects
- Teachers, trainers

Benefits:
- No trade license needed
- Simpler taxation
- Can work for multiple clients

Gewerbetreibender (Trader):
All other self-employment
- Need Gewerbeschein (trade license)
- Register with Gewerbeamt
- More regulations

WORK PERMIT WHILE STUDYING
--------------------------

Students (§16b residence permit):
- 140 full days OR 280 half days per year
- No limit for university-related jobs
- Must not delay studies

After graduation:
- 18 months to find qualified job
- Can work any job during search
- Must relate to studies once found
