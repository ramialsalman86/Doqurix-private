SOCIAL SECURITY IN GERMANY (SOZIALVERSICHERUNG)
================================================
Source: Deutsche Rentenversicherung, Bundesagentur für Arbeit
Last Updated: January 2026

OVERVIEW - THE 5 PILLARS
------------------------
German social security consists of 5 mandatory insurances:

1. Health Insurance (Krankenversicherung)
2. Pension Insurance (Rentenversicherung)
3. Unemployment Insurance (Arbeitslosenversicherung)
4. Long-term Care Insurance (Pflegeversicherung)
5. Accident Insurance (Unfallversicherung)

Employees contribute to all 5 (except accident - paid by employer)
Self-employed have different rules

CONTRIBUTION RATES 2024/2025
----------------------------
(Split 50/50 between employer and employee, except where noted)

Insurance Type          | Total Rate | Employee | Employer
------------------------|------------|----------|----------
Health (GKV)            | ~15.9%     | ~7.95%   | ~7.95%
Pension                 | 18.6%      | 9.3%     | 9.3%
Unemployment            | 2.6%       | 1.3%     | 1.3%
Long-term Care          | 3.4%       | 1.7%*    | 1.7%
Accident                | ~1.3%      | 0%       | 100%

*Long-term care: Additional 0.6% for childless employees over 23

CONTRIBUTION CEILINGS 2024
--------------------------
Beitragsbemessungsgrenze (assessment ceiling):

Health/Long-term Care:
- West & East Germany: €62,100/year (€5,175/month)

Pension/Unemployment:
- West Germany: €90,600/year (€7,550/month)
- East Germany: €89,400/year (€7,450/month)

Income above these limits is not subject to contributions

SOZIALVERSICHERUNGSAUSWEIS (SOCIAL SECURITY CARD)
-------------------------------------------------
What is it?
- Official document with your Social Security Number
- Issued by Deutsche Rentenversicherung
- 12-digit number (Sozialversicherungsnummer)
- Format: XX DDMMYY X XXXX (includes birthdate)

When do you get it?
- Automatically when starting first job in Germany
- Employer triggers the process
- Arrives by mail within 4-6 weeks

Why do you need it?
- Required for every job
- Employer needs number to register you
- Proof of pension contributions

Lost your card?
- Request replacement from Rentenversicherung
- Online: www.deutsche-rentenversicherung.de
- Free of charge

PENSION INSURANCE (RENTENVERSICHERUNG)
--------------------------------------
Who pays?
- All employees (Pflichtversichert)
- Some self-employed (certain professions)
- Voluntary for others

Contribution: 18.6% (9.3% each employer/employee)

What you get:
- Old-age pension (Altersrente) at retirement age
- Disability pension (Erwerbsminderungsrente)
- Survivor's pension for dependents
- Rehabilitation services

Retirement age:
- Currently transitioning to 67 (by birth year)
- Born 1964 or later: 67 years
- Early retirement possible with deductions

Minimum for pension:
- 5 years of contributions (Wartezeit) for basic pension
- 35 years for early retirement options
- 45 years for pension without deductions

For foreigners:
- Contributions count toward pension
- Pension can be paid abroad (most countries)
- May transfer to home country pension system (some agreements)
- Request refund if leaving with less than 5 years? Complex rules

UNEMPLOYMENT INSURANCE (ARBEITSLOSENVERSICHERUNG)
-------------------------------------------------
Who pays?
- All employees
- Contribution: 2.6% (1.3% each)

To receive benefits (Arbeitslosengeld I):
- Must have worked 12 months in past 30 months
- Must be registered as unemployed at Agentur für Arbeit
- Must be available for work
- Must actively seek employment

Benefit amount:
- 60% of previous net salary (without children)
- 67% of previous net salary (with children)
- Maximum ~€3,000/month

Duration:
- Depends on contribution period and age
- 6-12 months for most people
- Up to 24 months for older workers

Arbeitslosengeld II (Bürgergeld):
- For those not eligible for ALG I
- Basic social welfare
- ~€563/month single person (2024) + housing
- Means-tested

LONG-TERM CARE INSURANCE (PFLEGEVERSICHERUNG)
---------------------------------------------
Who pays?
- All employees with health insurance
- Contribution: 3.4% (1.7% each)
- Additional 0.6% for childless employees over 23

What it covers:
- Home care services
- Nursing home costs (partial)
- Care for disabled family members
- Cash benefits for family caregivers

Care levels (Pflegegrade):
- Grade 1-5 based on care needs
- Assessment by MDK (Medical Service)
- Higher grade = more benefits

ACCIDENT INSURANCE (UNFALLVERSICHERUNG)
---------------------------------------
Who pays?
- 100% employer (not deducted from salary)
- Contribution: ~1.3% average

What it covers:
- Work-related accidents
- Commute accidents (to/from work)
- Occupational diseases
- Rehabilitation
- Disability pension if permanent injury

Provider:
- Berufsgenossenschaft (professional associations)
- Different BGs for different industries

MINI-JOB (GERINGFÜGIGE BESCHÄFTIGUNG)
-------------------------------------
What is it?
- Employment earning up to €538/month (2024)
- Also called €538-Job or Minijob

Social security for Mini-Jobs:
- No employee health insurance contribution
- No unemployment insurance
- Pension: Optional opt-out (Befreiung)
- Employer pays flat rates

Midi-Job (€538.01 - €2,000/month):
- Reduced employee contributions
- Full employer contributions
- Sliding scale (Gleitzone)

SELF-EMPLOYED (SELBSTSTÄNDIGE)
------------------------------
Social security for self-employed:

Mandatory:
- Health insurance (GKV or PKV)
- Long-term care insurance
- Some professions: Pension (artists, journalists, etc.)

Voluntary:
- Pension insurance
- Unemployment insurance (special conditions)

No employer contribution - you pay everything!

WORKING STUDENTS (WERKSTUDENTEN)
--------------------------------
Special rules for enrolled students:
- Working during semester: max 20 hours/week
- Exempt from unemployment, health, care insurance
- Only pension insurance applies
- During semester breaks: full-time allowed

INTERNATIONAL AGREEMENTS
------------------------
Germany has social security agreements with many countries:
- EU/EEA: Full coordination of benefits
- USA, Turkey, many others: Bilateral agreements
- Coverage periods may be combined

For EU citizens:
- Periods in other EU countries count
- No gap in coverage when moving
- Request A1 form for posted workers

IMPORTANT DOCUMENTS
-------------------
- Sozialversicherungsausweis: Social security card
- Lohnabrechnung: Pay slip showing contributions
- Renteninformation: Annual pension statement
- Jahresmeldung: Annual employer report
