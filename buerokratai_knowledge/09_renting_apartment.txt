RENTING AN APARTMENT IN GERMANY (WOHNUNGSSUCHE)
================================================
Source: Mieterbund, German Tenant Protection Laws
Last Updated: January 2026

OVERVIEW OF GERMAN RENTAL MARKET
--------------------------------
Germany has one of the highest tenant rates in Europe (~50% rent).
Rental market is highly competitive, especially in major cities.
Tenant protection is strong - landlords cannot easily evict.

TYPES OF RENTAL ACCOMMODATION
-----------------------------

1. Wohnung (Apartment)
   - Most common rental type
   - Can be furnished (möbliert) or unfurnished (unmöbliert)
   - Usually unfurnished means NO kitchen!

2. WG (Wohngemeinschaft) - Shared Apartment
   - Share apartment with others
   - Each person has own room
   - Share kitchen/bathroom
   - Popular with students and young professionals
   - Cheaper option

3. Apartment (Einzimmerwohnung)
   - Studio/one-room apartment
   - Living and sleeping in one room
   - Own kitchen and bathroom

4. Zimmer (Room)
   - Single room in someone else's apartment
   - May share facilities
   - Subtenant (Untermieter)

UNDERSTANDING GERMAN RENT
-------------------------

Kaltmiete (Cold Rent):
- Base rent without utilities
- What landlord receives

Nebenkosten (Additional Costs):
- Utilities: Heating, water, garbage, building insurance
- Usually paid as monthly advance (Vorauszahlung)
- Settled annually (Nebenkostenabrechnung)

Warmmiete (Warm Rent):
- Kaltmiete + Nebenkosten
- Total monthly payment
- What you actually pay

NOT typically included:
- Electricity (Strom) - separate contract with provider
- Internet/TV - separate contract
- Sometimes heating (check listing)

APARTMENT LISTINGS TERMINOLOGY
------------------------------
Common terms in listings:

Zimmer (Zi.): Number of rooms (excluding kitchen/bath)
qm or m²: Square meters
EG: Erdgeschoss (ground floor)
1.OG, 2.OG: 1st floor, 2nd floor (above ground)
DG: Dachgeschoss (top floor/attic)
KM: Kaltmiete (cold rent)
WM: Warmmiete (warm rent)
NK: Nebenkosten
Kaution: Security deposit
EBK: Einbauküche (fitted kitchen included)
provisionsfrei: No agent fee
möbliert: Furnished
unmöbliert: Unfurnished (usually no kitchen!)
Altbau: Old building (pre-1949, high ceilings)
Neubau: New building
Balkon: Balcony
Terrasse: Terrace
Keller: Basement storage

REQUIRED DOCUMENTS FOR RENTING
------------------------------
Landlords typically require:

1. Selbstauskunft (Self-disclosure form)
   - Personal information
   - Employment status
   - Income
   - Previous landlord contacts

2. Schufa-Auskunft (Credit report)
   - Get free annual copy: meineschufa.de
   - Or pay for immediate online access
   - Shows credit history and score

3. Proof of Income (Einkommensnachweis)
   - Last 3 pay slips (Gehaltsabrechnungen)
   - Employment contract
   - Rule of thumb: Rent should be <30-40% of net income

4. Copy of ID/Passport
   - Residence permit for non-EU citizens

5. Mietschuldenfreiheitsbescheinigung
   - Letter from previous landlord confirming no rent debts
   - Not always required, but helpful

6. Employment Contract
   - Shows stable employment

For newcomers without German documents:
- Bank statements from home country
- Employment contract for new German job
- Employer guarantee letter
- Larger deposit (if landlord agrees)

KAUTION (SECURITY DEPOSIT)
--------------------------
Legal rules:
- Maximum: 3 months cold rent (Kaltmiete)
- Must be kept in separate savings account
- Interest belongs to tenant
- Returned after move-out (can take months)
- Landlord can use for damages/unpaid rent

Payment options:
- One-time payment
- 3 monthly installments (legal right)
- Bank guarantee (Bankbürgschaft)
- Deposit insurance (Mietkautionsversicherung)

MIETVERTRAG (RENTAL CONTRACT)
-----------------------------
Types of contracts:

Unbefristeter Mietvertrag:
- Unlimited/indefinite term
- Most common
- Best for tenants
- Landlord needs valid reason to terminate

Befristeter Mietvertrag:
- Fixed term (e.g., 2 years)
- Must state valid reason for time limit
- Automatically becomes unlimited if no valid reason

Zwischenmiete:
- Sublease/temporary
- While main tenant is away
- Fixed term

TENANT RIGHTS IN GERMANY
------------------------
German law strongly protects tenants:

✓ Landlord needs valid reason to terminate (unlimited contracts)
✓ Notice period: Usually 3 months for tenant, 3-9 months for landlord
✓ Rent increase limits (Mietpreisbremse in many cities)
✓ Right to 3-month installments for deposit
✓ Landlord must give 24-hour notice to visit
✓ Cannot be evicted in winter without alternative housing
✓ Protected from arbitrary rent increases

MIETPREISBREMSE (RENT CONTROL)
------------------------------
In designated areas (most large cities):
- Rent for new tenants limited to 10% above local average
- Exceptions: New buildings, comprehensive renovation
- Check if your city has this: Usually Berlin, Munich, Hamburg, Frankfurt, etc.

KÜNDIGUNGSSCHUTZ (TERMINATION PROTECTION)
-----------------------------------------
Landlord can only terminate if:
- Personal use (Eigenbedarf) - needs apartment for self/family
- Significant contract violation by tenant
- Economic necessity (very rare)

Notice periods:
- Tenant: Always 3 months
- Landlord: 3 months (up to 5 years tenancy)
- Landlord: 6 months (5-8 years tenancy)
- Landlord: 9 months (over 8 years tenancy)

Housing companies (Wohnungsbaugesellschaften):
- City-owned companies
- Often cheaper
- Long waiting lists
- Apply directly: Search "[city name] Wohnungsbaugesellschaft"

VIEWINGS (WOHNUNGSBESICHTIGUNG)
-------------------------------
Tips for apartment viewings:

Prepare:
- Bring all documents
- Dress professionally
- Arrive on time
- Bring proof of income

Questions to ask:
- What's included in Nebenkosten?
- Who handles repairs?
- When is the apartment available?
- Are there any planned renovations?
- Rules about pets, subletting?

Be ready to:
- Decide quickly (apartments go fast)
- Make good impression (competition is fierce)
- Have documents ready immediately

WOHNUNGSGEBERBESTÄTIGUNG
------------------------
After moving in:
- Landlord MUST provide this within 2 weeks
- You need it for Anmeldung (address registration)
- It's legally required
- If landlord refuses: Report to Bürgeramt
