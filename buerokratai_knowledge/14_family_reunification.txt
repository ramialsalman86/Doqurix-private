FAMILY REUNIFICATION IN GERMANY (FAMILIENNACHZUG)
=================================================
Source: BAMF, German Embassy Guidelines
Last Updated: January 2026

OVERVIEW
--------
Family members of people living in Germany can apply to join them.
Rules differ for EU and non-EU citizens, and for different family members.

WHO CAN BRING FAMILY TO GERMANY?
--------------------------------

1. EU/EEA Citizens
   - Can bring family relatively easily
   - Family members get residence cards
   - Include: Spouse, children under 21, dependent parents

2. Non-EU Citizens with Residence Permit
   - Must have stable residence permit
   - Usually need sufficient income and housing
   - Different rules for different permit types

3. German Citizens
   - Can bring foreign spouse and children
   - Must prove relationship is genuine

REQUIREMENTS FOR SPONSOR (Person in Germany)
--------------------------------------------

1. Legal Residence
   - Settlement permit (Niederlassungserlaubnis) OR
   - Residence permit valid for at least one year OR
   - Blue Card OR
   - Certain other specific permits

2. Sufficient Living Space
   - At least 12 sqm per person
   - Must prove rental contract or ownership
   - Exception: For Blue Card holders - less strict

3. Sufficient Income
   - Must support family without social benefits
   - Varies by family size
   - Guideline: Net income covering basic needs

4. Health Insurance
   - Family must be covered by insurance
   - Usually sponsor's insurance extends to family

SPOUSE/PARTNER REUNIFICATION
----------------------------

Requirements:
1. Legal marriage (or registered partnership)
2. Both spouses at least 18 years old
3. Basic German knowledge (A1 level) - usually
4. Genuine marriage (not for immigration purposes)

Exceptions from German requirement:
- Spouse of German citizen (may be exempt)
- Spouse of Blue Card holder (may be exempt)
- Spouse cannot learn German due to disability
- Illiteracy in home country
- University degree

A1 German Test Options:
- Goethe-Zertifikat A1
- Start Deutsch 1
- telc Deutsch A1
- ÖSD Zertifikat A1

Take at Goethe-Institut or partner in home country.

CHILDREN (Kindernachzug)
------------------------

Minor Children (under 18):
- Both parents must agree
- No German requirement
- Must live with parent(s) in Germany

Children 16-18:
- May need German knowledge
- OR must show integration potential
- Stricter if only one parent in Germany

Adult Children (18+):
- Generally NOT eligible for family reunification
- Exception: Severe hardship cases

PARENTS (Elternnachzug)
-----------------------
Very restricted for third-country nationals:

Parents of Minor Child in Germany:
- If child is German citizen
- Parent has custody rights
- Best interests of child require it

Parents of Adult in Germany:
- Only in exceptional hardship cases
- Must prove dependence
- Rarely granted

EU/EEA FAMILY MEMBERS
---------------------
More liberal rules:

Family members of EU citizens:
- Spouse/registered partner
- Children under 21
- Dependent children over 21
- Dependent parents
- NO German language requirement
- NO income requirement for sponsor

Get EU family member residence card.

BLUE CARD FAMILY PRIVILEGES
---------------------------
Spouses of Blue Card holders:
- NO German language requirement!
- Can work immediately
- Fast track with Blue Card holder
- Path to permanent residence together

APPLICATION PROCESS
-------------------

Step 1: Family members apply at German Embassy
   - In their home country
   - Bring all required documents
   - Interview possible

Step 2: Foreigners' Office (Ausländerbehörde) in Germany
   - Must approve the application
   - Sponsor may need to provide documents
   - Can take several months

Step 3: Visa issued
   - Family member travels to Germany
   - Registers address (Anmeldung)
   - Applies for residence permit at local Ausländerbehörde

Timeline:
- Processing: 2-6 months (varies greatly)
- Blue Card families: Often faster
- Some countries: Longer due to volume

REQUIRED DOCUMENTS
------------------

For Sponsor (in Germany):
- Copy of passport
- Residence permit copy
- Employment contract
- Pay slips (last 3 months)
- Rental contract (Mietvertrag)
- Health insurance proof

For Family Member (abroad):
- Valid passport
- Marriage certificate (spouse)
- Birth certificate (children)
- German language certificate (if required)
- Passport photos
- Application form
- Proof of relationship

All documents usually need:
- Certified translations
- Apostille or legalization

COSTS
-----
Visa fee: €75 for adults, €37.50 for minors
Residence permit: €100-150
Language test: €80-150
Document translations: Varies

RESIDENCE PERMITS FOR FAMILY
----------------------------
Family members receive:
- Initially: Residence permit tied to sponsor's status
- Duration: Usually matches sponsor's permit
- Work: Usually allowed (check permit)
- Path to settlement permit after 5 years (with conditions)

INTEGRATION REQUIREMENTS
------------------------
Family members should/must:
- Attend integration course (often mandatory)
- Learn German to B1 level
- Participate in society

SEPARATION/DIVORCE
------------------
What happens if relationship ends:

Before 3 years in Germany:
- Family member may lose residence right
- Can apply for independent permit if:
  - Domestic violence occurred
  - Severe hardship would result

After 3 years in Germany:
- Independent residence permit possible
- If meets requirements (income, German, etc.)

SPECIAL CASES
-------------

Refugees (Recognized):
- Can bring nuclear family (spouse, minor children)
- Simplified process in first 3 months
- May be exempt from income/housing requirements

Subsidiary Protection:
- Family reunification suspended until March 2024
- Then: Monthly quota (1,000)
- Limited rights

COMMON MISTAKES TO AVOID
------------------------
✗ Applying without proper documents
✗ Forgetting to legalize/apostille documents
✗ Not getting documents translated
✗ Applying too late (before A1 certificate)
✗ Not having sufficient income proof
✗ Not declaring previous marriages
✗ Arriving on tourist visa hoping to convert
