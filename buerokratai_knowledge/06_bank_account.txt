BANK ACCOUNT IN GERMANY (GIROKONTO)
====================================
Source: BaFin, German Banking Association
Last Updated: January 2026

WHY YOU NEED A GERMAN BANK ACCOUNT
----------------------------------
- Salary payments (most employers require it)
- Rent payments (Überweisung/SEPA)
- Utility bills (direct debit/Lastschrift)
- Health insurance payments
- Required for apartment rental applications
- Some landlords refuse without German account

LEGAL RIGHT TO BASIC ACCOUNT
----------------------------
Since 2016, everyone legally residing in EU has right to basic account
(Basiskonto) under Payment Accounts Directive.
- Banks CANNOT refuse you for nationality
- Banks CANNOT refuse for lack of income
- Must provide essential services

TYPES OF BANK ACCOUNTS
----------------------

1. GIROKONTO (Current Account)
   - Standard everyday account
   - Salary deposits
   - Direct debits (Lastschrift)
   - Transfers (Überweisungen)
   - Usually comes with debit card (Girocard)

2. BASISKONTO (Basic Account)
   - For those who can't get regular Girokonto
   - Limited features but covers essentials
   - Right guaranteed by law

3. SPARKONTO (Savings Account)
   - For saving money
   - Higher interest (usually)
   - Limited withdrawals

4. TAGESGELDKONTO (Call Money Account)
   - Savings with daily access
   - Better interest than Girokonto
   - No direct payment functions

TYPES OF BANKS IN GERMANY
-------------------------

Traditional Banks:
- Deutsche Bank - largest private bank
- Commerzbank - major private bank
- Sparkasse - public savings banks (local)
- Volksbank/Raiffeisenbank - cooperative banks (local)

Direct/Online Banks:
- ING Germany - popular, good English
- DKB (Deutsche Kreditbank) - good for traveling
- Comdirect - part of Commerzbank
- Consorsbank

Neobanks/Fintech (app-based):
- N26 - popular with expats, English interface
- Tomorrow - sustainable banking
- Vivid Money
- Revolut (UK-based, limited German features)

                 | Traditional | Online     | Neobank
-----------------|-------------|------------|----------

English support  | Limited     | Good       | Excellent

REQUIRED DOCUMENTS
------------------
For most banks:
1. Valid passport or ID card
2. Anmeldung (address registration) - USUALLY REQUIRED
3. Tax ID (Steuer-ID) - some banks need it
4. Visa/residence permit (non-EU citizens)
5. Proof of employment or income (sometimes)

Some banks accept:
- Hotel booking (temporary address)
- Employer letter
- No Anmeldung (limited cases)

BANKS THAT WORK WITHOUT ANMELDUNG
---------------------------------
For newcomers before address registration:
- N26 - can open with passport only
- Tomorrow - flexible requirements
- Vivid - flexible requirements
- Some accounts possible with temporary address

Note: These accounts may have limitations initially

ACCOUNT OPENING PROCESS
-----------------------

Traditional Bank (in branch):
1. Book appointment
2. Bring documents
3. Sign application in person
4. Receive card by mail (1-2 weeks)
5. PIN sent separately

Online Bank (video identification):
1. Apply online
2. Video call for identification (5-15 min)
3. Or PostIdent at post office
4. Account active within 1-3 days
5. Card arrives by mail

Neobank (app-based):
1. Download app
2. Register and upload documents
3. Video identification in app
4. Account active within hours to days
5. Card ordered through app

ACCOUNT FEES
------------
Common fees to watch:
- Monthly account fee (Kontoführungsgebühr): €0-15/month
- Card fee (Kartengebühr): €0-30/year for Girocard
- Credit card fee: €0-100/year
- ATM withdrawals: Free at own network, €2-5 elsewhere
- International transfers: €5-50 depending on method

Free accounts available at:
- DKB (free with salary deposit)
- ING (free with €700/month deposit)
- N26 (basic account)
- Comdirect

GIROCARD VS. CREDIT CARD
------------------------
Girocard (EC-Karte):
- Debit card for German payment system
- Money deducted immediately
- Works at most German shops
- ATM withdrawals at bank's network
- NOT Visa/Mastercard

Credit Card:
- Visa or Mastercard
- Works internationally
- Monthly billing
- Often extra fee in Germany
- Not always accepted at German shops!

Recommendation: Have both!

SCHUFA - GERMAN CREDIT SCORE
----------------------------
What is Schufa?
- Credit reporting agency
- Tracks credit history in Germany
- Score from 0-100% (higher is better)
- Banks check Schufa when opening account

For newcomers:
- No Schufa history initially
- Some banks reject without Schufa
- N26, Tomorrow more flexible
- Build history: Get phone contract, pay on time

Check your Schufa:
- Free annual report: www.meineschufa.de (Datenkopie)
- Paid detailed report available

IMPORTANT BANKING TERMS
-----------------------
- IBAN: International Bank Account Number (DE + 20 digits)
- BIC/SWIFT: Bank identification for international transfers
- Überweisung: Bank transfer
- Lastschrift: Direct debit (automatic withdrawal)
- Dauerauftrag: Standing order (regular transfers)
- Kontoauszug: Bank statement
- Girokonto: Current account
- PIN: Personal identification number
- TAN: Transaction authentication number

BLOCKED ACCOUNT (SPERRKONTO)
----------------------------
For visa applications:

What is it?
- Special savings account with limited withdrawals
- Required for student visas and some other visas
- Proves you can support yourself in Germany

Requirements 2024/2025:
- Student visa: €11,208/year (€934/month)
- Amount blocked, released monthly

Providers:
- Deutsche Bank
- Fintiba (digital, popular)
- Expatrio (digital, popular)
- Coracle

Timeline:
- Fintiba/Expatrio: 3-5 days
- Deutsche Bank: 2-4 weeks

TIPS FOR EXPATS
---------------
1. Open account early (even before arrival if possible)
2. Get N26 or similar as backup
3. Have some cash for first days
4. Set up direct debits for rent/utilities
5. Get both Girocard AND credit card
6. Keep your address updated with bank
7. Check fees carefully
