SECTION 1: VAT RATES AND BASIC PRINCIPLES
============================================================

STANDARD VAT RATE: 19%
Applies to most goods and services in Germany unless specifically exempted or eligible for reduced rate. This is the default rate for any commercial transaction.

Examples of standard-rated items:
- Electronics and appliances
- Clothing and footwear
- Furniture and home goods
- Motor vehicles
- Restaurant meals and catering
- Hotels (accommodation portion)
- Entertainment and leisure services
- Professional services (consulting, legal, accounting)
- Construction and renovation services
- Most digital services

REDUCED VAT RATE: 7%
Applied to specific categories deemed essential or culturally important:

Food and Beverages:
- Most food products for home consumption
- Milk, dairy products, eggs
- Meat, fish, and poultry
- Fruits and vegetables
- Bread, cereals, and grains
- Coffee, tea, and non-alcoholic beverages
- EXCEPTION: Restaurant meals are 19%
- EXCEPTION: Alcoholic beverages are 19%
- EXCEPTION: Luxury foods may be 19%

Cultural Goods and Services:
- Books (physical and e-books since 2020)
- Newspapers and magazines
- Admission to museums and exhibitions
- Theater and concert tickets
- Admission to zoos and botanical gardens
- Audiobooks and e-publications

Transportation:
- Public transport tickets within Germany
- Regional and local train services
- Tram, bus, and metro services
- EXCEPTION: Long-distance trains (ICE) = 19%
- EXCEPTION: Taxis = 7%

Medical and Health:
- Certain medical supplies and equipment
- Prosthetics and medical aids
- Dental work (partial)
- EXCEPTION: Most medical services are VAT-exempt

Hospitality:
- Hotel accommodation (overnight stays)
- Campsite fees
- EXCEPTION: Hotel breakfast and minibar = 19%

Other Reduced-Rate Items:
- Agricultural products when sold by farmers
- Live animals
- Cut flowers and plants
- Certain artworks sold by artists
- Water supply for household use

ZERO RATE (0% VAT):
Applied to specific transactions where VAT is technically charged but at 0%:

Export Deliveries:
- Goods shipped outside the EU
- With proper export documentation
- Must prove goods left Germany

Photovoltaic Systems:
- NEW from 2023: 0% VAT on installation of solar panels
- Applies to rooftop and building-attached systems
- Includes all related equipment (batteries, inverters)
- For residential buildings with up to 30 kW capacity
- Major incentive for renewable energy adoption

Intra-EU Supply of Goods:
- B2B sales to other EU countries
- Buyer must have valid VAT ID
- Goods must physically move to another EU country
- Seller must report on EC Sales List
- Reverse charge applies (buyer pays VAT in their country)

International Transport:
- Certain international shipping services
- Cross-border freight transport

HISTORICAL NOTE - COVID-19 TEMPORARY REDUCTION:
From July 1, 2020 to December 31, 2020:
- Standard rate temporarily reduced to 16%
- Reduced rate temporarily reduced to 5%
- Measure to stimulate economy during pandemic
- Has since returned to normal rates

SECTION 2: VAT REGISTRATION THRESHOLDS
============================================================

SMALL BUSINESS REGULATION (Kleinunternehmerregelung):
The most important threshold for small businesses and freelancers.

Eligibility Criteria:
- Previous calendar year revenue: ≤ €22,000 (updated from €17,500 in 2020)
- Current calendar year expected revenue: ≤ €50,000
- Must meet BOTH conditions

Benefits of Small Business Status:
- Exempt from charging VAT on invoices
- No VAT returns required (no monthly/quarterly filing)
- Significantly reduced administrative burden
- Simpler accounting and bookkeeping
- Can offer lower prices to end consumers

Drawbacks of Small Business Status:
- Cannot deduct input VAT on purchases
- Disadvantageous when customers are businesses that can deduct VAT
- Disadvantageous when making large business investments
- Must state exemption status on all invoices
- May appear less professional to some clients

MANDATORY STATEMENT ON INVOICES:
Small businesses must include text such as:
"Umsatzsteuerbefreiung nach §19 UStG" 
(VAT exemption according to §19 VAT Act)

OPTIONAL OPT-OUT:
- Small businesses CAN voluntarily choose regular VAT taxation
- Must notify tax office in writing
- Bound by decision for 5 calendar years minimum
- Cannot switch back to small business status during this period
- Beneficial if:
  - Significant input VAT on purchases/investments
  - Customers are primarily VAT-registered businesses
  - Large startup investments planned

FIRST YEAR SPECIAL RULE:
- In founding year, estimate full-year revenue
- Tax office may require quarterly reporting initially
- After year-end, actual revenue determines ongoing status

DOMESTIC BUSINESS REGISTRATION:
For businesses above small business threshold:
- Must register for VAT if annual revenue exceeds €22,000
- Registration with local tax office (Finanzamt)
- Receive VAT identification number (Umsatzsteuer-Identifikationsnummer)
- Must begin filing VAT returns

FOREIGN BUSINESSES:
No threshold - must register immediately if:
- Providing taxable supplies in Germany
- Storing goods in Germany for later sale
- Having taxable transactions subject to German VAT

EU Businesses - Distance Selling:
- Threshold for distance sales to Germany: €100,000 annually
- Can use One-Stop-Shop (OSS) system for simplified compliance
- Combined threshold across all EU member states
- Applies to online sales to consumers

NON-EU BUSINESSES:
- Must appoint fiscal representative in Germany
- No threshold - registration required from first transaction
- Fiscal representative responsible for VAT compliance
- Can apply for VAT refunds through special procedure

SECTION 3: VAT-EXEMPT GOODS AND SERVICES
============================================================

FINANCIAL SERVICES (§4 Nr. 8 UStG):
Comprehensive exemption for financial sector:

Banking Services:
- Granting and management of loans and credit
- Operation of bank accounts
- Payment processing services
- Money transfer services
- Foreign exchange transactions
- Debt collection services
- Credit intermediation

Investment Services:
- Investment fund management
- Portfolio management
- Custody services for securities
- Advisory services for investments (specific types)

Insurance:
- All insurance transactions
- Insurance agent services
- Reinsurance services

Exceptions (Taxable):
- Safe deposit box rental
- Debt collection on own account
- Financial consulting (some types)

MEDICAL AND HEALTH SERVICES (§4 Nr. 14 UStG):
Essential healthcare services exempt:

Medical Services:
- Doctor consultations and treatment
- Hospital services
- Dental treatment (diagnostic and therapeutic)
- Physiotherapy (with medical prescription)
- Psychotherapy
- Midwife services
- Medical laboratory services
- Ambulance services

Conditions:
- Services must be performed by qualified medical professionals
- Within scope of professional authorization
- For diagnostic, therapeutic, or preventive purposes

Taxable Medical Services:
- Cosmetic surgery (non-medical)
- Beauty treatments
- Wellness services
- Sports medical examinations (non-diagnostic)

EDUCATION SERVICES (§4 Nr. 21 UStG):
Educational services broadly exempt:

General Education:
- Primary and secondary schools
- Universities and colleges
- Vocational training institutions
- Language schools (if recognized)
- Tutoring services (with teaching qualification)

Conditions:
- Must be provided by qualified educators
- Recognized educational institution or qualification
- Must have educational purpose

Taxable Educational Services:
- Corporate training by commercial providers
- Training without recognized qualification
- Sports instruction
- Hobby courses and workshops

REAL ESTATE (§4 Nr. 12 UStG):
Complex rules for property transactions:

VAT-Exempt:
- Long-term rental of residential property (>6 months)
- Long-term rental of commercial property (option to tax available)
- Sale of land (bare land)
- Sale of buildings
- Sale of previously used buildings

VAT-Taxable:
- Short-term accommodation (hotels, vacation rentals)
- Parking space rental
- Storage space rental
- First sale of new buildings (within 5 years)
- Sale with option to tax exercised

Option to Tax (§9 UStG):
- Landlord can voluntarily choose VAT taxation
- Only for commercial property
- Beneficial if tenant can deduct input VAT
- Must notify tax office
- Cannot be revoked for existing lease

CULTURAL AND PUBLIC SERVICES:
Services by Public Institutions:
- Public theaters and opera houses
- State-run museums
- Public zoos and botanical gardens
- Public libraries
- Certain sporting events

Conditions:
- Must be provided by public bodies
- Within public service mandate
- Not in direct competition with private sector

OTHER IMPORTANT EXEMPTIONS:

Postal Services:
- Universal postal services by Deutsche Post
- Letter delivery within public mandate

Gambling and Lotteries:
- Exempt from VAT
- Subject to separate gambling tax instead

Social Services:
- Care services for elderly and disabled
- Child daycare services
- Social welfare services
- Services by recognized charitable organizations

Religious Services:
- Services by churches and religious organizations
- Within religious context

Medical Supplies:
- Certain prosthetics and aids
- When prescribed and medically necessary

IMPORTANT NOTE ON EXEMPTIONS:
- Exempt supplies do NOT allow input VAT deduction
- Businesses making only exempt supplies cannot deduct VAT on costs
- Can significantly increase effective costs
- Exception: Export sales (0% rated but allows input VAT deduction)

SECTION 4: REVERSE CHARGE MECHANISM
============================================================

DOMESTIC REVERSE CHARGE (§13b UStG):
Shifts VAT liability from supplier to recipient in specific sectors to combat fraud.

APPLICABLE SECTORS:

Construction Services:
- Building construction and civil engineering
- Installation work
- Completion and finishing work
- Building cleaning services
Conditions:
- Both parties registered for VAT
- Recipient is also in construction sector
- Service directly related to construction work

Scrap Metal and Waste Materials:
- Scrap metal and waste material deliveries
- Precious metals and certain raw materials
- Anti-fraud measure in high-risk sector

Emissions Allowances:
- Trading of CO2 emissions certificates
- Gas and electricity trading certificates

Gas and Electricity:
- Supply of gas through natural gas network
- Supply of electricity
- Between businesses only

Construction Machinery Rental:
- Rental of machinery with operator for construction
- If operator serves only auxiliary function

Services by Foreign Entrepreneurs:
- Any service provided by non-German business to German business
- Most common domestic reverse charge scenario
- Applies automatically if supplier has no German VAT registration

HOW DOMESTIC REVERSE CHARGE WORKS:

Traditional VAT (Normal Case):
1. Supplier charges 19% VAT on invoice
2. Supplier pays VAT to tax office
3. Customer deducts input VAT

With Reverse Charge:
1. Supplier invoices net amount (0% VAT)
2. Customer self-assesses VAT
3. Customer reports VAT as output tax
4. Customer immediately deducts as input tax
5. Net effect: Usually zero VAT payment

INVOICE REQUIREMENTS FOR REVERSE CHARGE:
Supplier must include statement such as:
"Steuerschuldnerschaft des Leistungsempfängers"
(Tax liability of service recipient)
or
"Reverse Charge - Leistungsempfänger schuldet die Steuer"

CROSS-BORDER REVERSE CHARGE:
Applies to B2B services from abroad to German businesses.

General Rule:
- Foreign supplier does not charge German VAT
- German customer self-assesses VAT
- Reported on customer's VAT return
- Usually immediately deductible as input VAT

Examples:
- Consulting services from UK company to German GmbH
- Software licenses from US company to German business
- Marketing services from French company to German company
- Technical support from any foreign company

Requirements:
- Recipient must have valid German VAT ID
- Service must be B2B (not to consumers)
- Place of supply rules determine it's taxable in Germany

PLACE OF SUPPLY RULES (Simplified):
- Services generally taxable where customer is established
- Exception: Services related to real estate (where property located)
- Exception: Transport services (based on distance traveled)
- Exception: Events and cultural services (where event takes place)

REPORTING:
Both domestic and cross-border reverse charges reported on VAT return:
- Box 46: Reverse charge VAT owed
- Box 47: Reverse charge input VAT deducted
- If equal, no net payment

PENALTIES FOR NON-COMPLIANCE:
- Failure to apply reverse charge when required
- Incorrect invoicing
- Can result in VAT assessment
- Interest and penalties
- Both supplier and customer may be liable

SECTION 5: INPUT VAT DEDUCTION RULES
============================================================

GENERAL PRINCIPLE:
VAT-registered businesses can deduct VAT paid on business inputs from VAT collected on outputs. This ensures VAT is only borne by final consumers.

BASIC REQUIREMENTS FOR DEDUCTION:

1. Valid Tax Invoice:
Must contain:
- Supplier's complete name and address
- Supplier's VAT identification number (USt-IdNr.)
- Customer's complete name and address
- Invoice date and unique invoice number
- Description of goods/services supplied
- Quantity and nature of goods/extent of services
- Date of supply or advance payment
- Net amount for each item
- Applicable VAT rate (7%, 19%, or reverse charge)
- Gross VAT amount
- Reference to exemption if applicable

2. Business Purpose:
- Input must relate to taxable business activity
- Must be used for making taxable supplies
- Cannot be for private use or exempt supplies

3. Proper Documentation:
- Invoice must be received
- Payment documentation helpful but not required for deduction
- Must retain for 10 years

TIMING OF DEDUCTION:
- Deductible in period invoice received
- Or in period service performed if invoice later
- Can correct in later period if missed initially
- No time limit for correction (but interest may apply)

NON-DEDUCTIBLE INPUT VAT:

Purchases for Exempt Supplies:
- Cannot deduct VAT on costs related to exempt supplies
- Applies to: Banking, insurance, medical services, education
- Must allocate costs if making both taxable and exempt supplies

Private Use or Mixed Use:
- Private use: No deduction allowed
- Mixed use: Proportional deduction based on business use percentage
- Must maintain records proving business use percentage
- Annual adjustment if usage changes

Company Cars:
- Complex rules depending on usage
- 100% business use: 100% deduction
- Partial private use: Limited deduction
- Must maintain logbook if claiming >50% business use
- Private use portion taxed as benefit in kind

Entertainment and Hospitality:
- Business meals: 70% input VAT deductible (30% non-deductible)
- Event costs: Full deduction if clearly business purpose
- Customer entertainment: Limited deductibility
- Gifts: VAT deductible if value <€35 per recipient per year

Specific Non-Deductible Items:
- Certain luxury items
- Purchases from non-VAT-registered suppliers
- Invoices not meeting formal requirements
- Costs related to exempt supplies

PARTIAL DEDUCTION (Pro-Rata):
For businesses making both taxable and exempt supplies:

Calculation Method:
Input VAT Deductible = Total Input VAT × (Taxable Turnover ÷ Total Turnover)

Example:
- Total input VAT: €10,000
- Taxable turnover: €300,000
- Exempt turnover: €100,000
- Total turnover: €400,000
- Deductible: €10,000 × (€300,000 ÷ €400,000) = €7,500
- Non-deductible: €2,500

Alternative: Direct Allocation:
- Can directly allocate costs if clear relationship
- More accurate but more administrative burden
- Must be consistent and documented

ADJUSTMENTS:
Required when use of asset changes:

Capital Goods Adjustment (§15a UStG):
- Real estate: 10-year adjustment period
- Other capital goods >€1,000: 5-year adjustment period
- Must adjust if business use changes by more than 10%
- Annual adjustment calculation required

Example:
- Purchased equipment for €10,000 + €1,900 VAT
- Initially 100% business use - deducted €1,900
- Year 3: Use drops to 60% business
- Must repay: €1,900 × 40% × 3/5 = €456

SMALL ITEMS EXEMPTION:
Items ≤ €1,000 net value (§15a UStG):
- No adjustment obligation
- Simplification for low-value items
- Can expense immediately

INVOICE REQUIREMENTS DETAIL:

Simplified Invoices (Kleinbetragsrechnung):
For amounts up to €250 gross:
- Supplier's name and address
- Invoice date
- Description of supply
- Gross amount
- VAT rate
- No VAT ID required
- No customer name required

Common examples: Receipts for fuel, parking, small supplies

Standard Invoices (>€250):
All elements listed under "Valid Tax Invoice" required.

ELECTRONIC INVOICES (E-Rechnungen):
NEW REQUIREMENT from January 1, 2025:
- Mandatory for B2B transactions between German businesses
- Must be in structured electronic format
- PDF alone no longer sufficient (transitional period)
- Formats: XRechnung, ZUGFeRD, or equivalent
- Must ensure authenticity and integrity

Transitional Rules:
- Until December 31, 2026: PDF invoices still accepted
- Small businesses (≤€800,000): Extended until December 31, 2027
- EDI format users: Extended until December 31, 2027

Benefits:
- Automated processing
- Reduced errors
- Faster payment processing
- Better auditability

INPUT VAT REFUND FOR NON-GERMAN BUSINESSES:

EU Businesses:
- Can reclaim German VAT through home country portal
- Deadline: September 30 of following year
- No German VAT registration needed if no establishment
- Processed electronically

Non-EU Businesses:
- Special refund procedure
- Must apply through German tax office
- Requires reciprocity (Germany has similar right in applicant's country)
- More complex process
- Longer processing times

COMMON MISTAKES:
- Deducting VAT from invoices without proper VAT ID
- Missing invoice elements
- Deducting VAT from small business suppliers
- Not maintaining proper documentation
- Mixing private and business use without allocation
- Not performing required adjustments

SECTION 6: VAT RETURN FILING REQUIREMENTS
============================================================

FILING FREQUENCY DETERMINATION:

MONTHLY FILING (Monatlich):
Required if:
- Previous calendar year VAT liability > €9,000
- Applies to most medium and large businesses
- Due date: 10th day of following month
- 12 advance returns per year
- Plus annual VAT return

QUARTERLY FILING (Vierteljährlich):
Required if:
- Previous calendar year VAT liability between €1,000 and €9,000
  (Some sources cite €2,000-€9,000 threshold)
- Common for small to medium businesses
- Due date: 10th day of month following quarter
- 4 advance returns per year:
  - Q1 (Jan-Mar): Due April 10
  - Q2 (Apr-Jun): Due July 10
  - Q3 (Jul-Sep): Due October 10
  - Q4 (Oct-Dec): Due January 10
- Plus annual VAT return

ANNUAL ONLY:
Allowed if:
- Previous calendar year VAT liability < €1,000 (or €2,000)
- Only annual return required, no advance returns
- Due date: July 31 of following year (extended with tax advisor)
- Smallest administrative burden

SPECIAL CASES:

First Year of Business:
- MANDATORY monthly filing for entire first calendar year
- ALSO mandatory monthly for following calendar year
- Intended for close monitoring by tax authorities
- Cannot opt for quarterly even if below threshold
- After two years, standard thresholds apply

Permanent Extension Available:
- Can apply for one-month extension
- Must set up automatic payment of 1/11 of previous year's VAT
- Extends deadline from 10th to end of month
- Particularly useful for monthly filers
- Provides breathing room for accurate completion

FILING DEADLINES:

Advance Returns (Monthly/Quarterly):
- Standard: 10th of following month/quarter
- With extension: End of following month/quarter
- Electronic filing mandatory (ELSTER system)
- Payment due same day as filing deadline
- Late filing penalties apply

Annual VAT Return:
WITHOUT TAX ADVISOR:
- Standard deadline: July 31 of following year
- 2024 return: Due July 31, 2025
- 2025 return: Due July 31, 2026

WITH TAX ADVISOR:
- Extended deadline: End of February of second following year
- 2024 return: Due April 30, 2026 (special extension)
- 2025 return: Due February 28, 2027
- 2026 returns onwards: February 28/29 of second following year

ANNUAL RETURN REQUIREMENTS:
- Always required regardless of advance filing frequency
- Reconciles all advance payments with actual liability
- More detailed than advance returns
- Can result in additional payment or refund
- Must be filed even if no activity during year

ELECTRONIC FILING (ELSTER):

ELSTER System:
- ELSTER = Elektronische Steuererklärung
- Government-provided free software
- Mandatory for all VAT returns (advance and annual)
- Website: www.elster.de
- Available for Windows, Mac, and online

Registration Process:
1. Register online at www.elster.de
2. Provide personal/business information
3. Receive certificate by mail (takes 1-2 weeks)
4. Activate certificate online
5. Can then file returns electronically

Alternative Software:
- Commercial tax software can interface with ELSTER
- Popular options: DATEV, Lexware, WISO
- More user-friendly but often paid
- Still submits through ELSTER system

Filing Process:
1. Complete VAT return form (usually USt1A for advance, USt for annual)
2. Review and validate data
3. Submit electronically through ELSTER
4. Receive confirmation of submission
5. Tax office processes and assesses

PAYMENT REQUIREMENTS:

Payment Methods:
- Direct debit (SEPA-Lastschriftmandat) - recommended
- Bank transfer to tax office account
- Payment must be received by due date
- Reference number: Tax number + period

Automatic Direct Debit:
- Set up SEPA mandate with tax office
- Tax office automatically debits account
- Usually 1-2 weeks after return filed
- Most convenient method
- Reduces risk of late payment

Underpayment in Advance Returns:
- Interest charged: 0.5% per month = 6% annually
- If underpayment > €1,000 compared to actual liability
- Calculated from due date until payment
- Can be substantial for large amounts

Overpayment:
- Refunded after annual return filed
- Can request earlier refund in some cases
- Interest paid: 0.5% per month (after 15 months)

INTRASTAT REPORTING:
Additional reporting for intra-EU trade:

Required if:
- Dispatches (sales to EU): Annual value > €1,000,000
- Arrivals (purchases from EU): Annual value > €3,000,000

Filing Frequency:
- Monthly if above thresholds
- Separate from VAT return
- Deadline: 25th of following month
- More detailed than VAT reporting

Information Required:
- Commodity codes (8-digit)
- Country of origin/destination
- Value and quantity
- Mode of transport
- Transaction type

EC SALES LIST (ESL/ZM - Zusammenfassende Meldung):
Report of intra-EU B2B supplies:

Required for:
- All intra-EU B2B supplies of goods
- Intra-EU B2B services where reverse charge applies
- No minimum threshold

Filing Frequency:
- Monthly if annual goods dispatches > €100,000
- Otherwise quarterly
- Services: Always quarterly
- Deadline: 25th of month following period

Information Required:
- Customer's VAT ID
- Customer's country
- Total net value per customer
- Separate reporting for goods vs. services

PENALTIES FOR LATE FILING:

Late Filing Penalty:
- Automatic penalty for late submission
- €25 per month (or part month) started
- Can be up to 10% of VAT due
- Minimum penalty: €25
- Applied even for nil returns

Late Payment Interest:
- 0.5% per month = 6% annually
- Calculated from due date
- Applied to amount unpaid
- Can accumulate significantly

Repeated Late Filing:
- Increased scrutiny from tax office
- Potential forced quarterly/monthly filing
- May affect trust relationship with tax office
- Can lead to tax audits

CORRECTIONS AND AMENDMENTS:

Correcting Errors:
- Can correct in next return if amount < €1,000
- Must file amended return if amount > €1,000
- No time limit for voluntary correction
- Reduces or eliminates penalties

Voluntary Disclosure:
- If error discovered before tax office
- File corrected return as soon as possible
- Usually no penalties if truly voluntary
- Interest still may apply

TAX OFFICE AUDIT:
- VAT returns subject to review
- Tax office can request additional documentation
- Typically covers last 3-5 years
- Can extend to 10 years if fraud suspected
- Keep all records for 10 years minimum

RECORD KEEPING REQUIREMENTS:

Must Retain for 10 Years:
- All invoices issued
- All invoices received
- VAT return copies
- Payment documentation
- Import/export documents
- Contracts and agreements
- Accounting records

Format:
- Paper or electronic both acceptable
- Electronic records must be readable
- Must produce within reasonable time if requested
- GoBD rules apply (German electronic record keeping rules)

SECTION 7: ONE-STOP-SHOP (OSS) SYSTEM
============================================================

PURPOSE OF OSS:
Simplifies VAT compliance for cross-border B2C sales within the EU. Instead of registering for VAT in every EU country where you sell, register once in your home country and report all EU sales through a single portal.

TYPES OF OSS:

1. EU OSS (Union Scheme):
For businesses established in EU selling to consumers in other EU countries:
- Distance sales of goods
- Electronically supplied services (e-services)
- Telecoms, broadcasting, e-services to consumers
- No physical presence needed in customer's country

2. NON-EU OSS:
For businesses established outside EU:
- Selling e-services to EU consumers
- Can register in any EU member state
- Germany often chosen due to central location

3. IMPORT ONE-STOP-SHOP (IOSS):
For distance sales of imported goods:
- Goods from outside EU valued ≤ €150
- Seller collects VAT at point of sale
- Included in purchase price
- Customer pays no VAT at customs
- Speeds up customs clearance

HOW EU OSS WORKS:

Registration:
- Register through German tax portal (BZSt - Bundeszentralamt für Steuern)
- Receive OSS identification number
- Different from regular German VAT ID
- Can register even if no regular German VAT ID

Filing:
- Single quarterly return covering all EU sales
- Due by end of month following quarter:
  - Q1: Due April 30
  - Q2: Due July 31
  - Q3: Due October 31
  - Q4: Due January 31
- Report sales per EU country and VAT rate
- Calculate VAT using destination country's rates

Payment:
- Single payment to German tax authorities
- Germany distributes VAT to respective countries
- Payment in EUR
- Due same day as return

Advantages:
- No need to register in 27 EU countries
- Single return instead of multiple
- One contact point for questions
- Significantly reduced admin burden
- Simplified accounting

ELIGIBILITY AND THRESHOLDS:

Distance Selling Threshold:
- €10,000 annually across all EU countries (combined)
- Below threshold: Can charge home country VAT
- Above threshold: Must charge destination country VAT
- Can opt into OSS even below threshold (recommended)

Goods Covered:
- Physical goods shipped from Germany to EU consumers
- No goods physically present in destination country
- Marketplace facilitation (online platforms)

Services Covered:
- Telecommunications services
- Broadcasting services
- Electronically supplied services:
  - Software, apps, games
  - Streaming services
  - E-books and digital publications
  - Website services
  - Cloud services
  - Digital content

REQUIREMENTS:

Records to Keep:
- Member state of consumer identification
- Type of services/goods supplied
- Date and amount of sale
- VAT rate applied and amount collected
- Retain for 10 years
- Must be available electronically

Consumer Location Evidence:
Need two non-contradictory pieces of evidence:
- Billing address
- Bank account location
- IP address
- SIM card country code
- Delivery address (for goods)

VAT RATES TO APPLY:
Must apply destination country VAT rates:
- Germany: 19% standard, 7% reduced
- France: 20% standard, 5.5%/10% reduced
- Poland: 23% standard, 8%/5% reduced
- Austria: 20% standard, 10%/13% reduced
- And so on for all 27 EU countries

IMPORT OSS (IOSS):

Who Should Use:
- Online retailers shipping from outside EU
- Marketplaces facilitating such sales
- Goods value ≤ €150
- B2C sales only

Benefits for Consumers:
- VAT included in purchase price
- No unexpected charges at delivery
- Faster delivery (no customs delay)
- Better customer experience

Benefits for Sellers:
- Competitive advantage
- Fewer cart abandonments
- One VAT registration instead of 27
- Simplified compliance

How It Works:
1. Register for IOSS in Germany (or any EU country)
2. Collect VAT at checkout using destination country rate
3. File monthly IOSS return (not quarterly)
4. Report per country and VAT rate
5. Germany distributes VAT to countries
6. Customs clearance simplified with IOSS number

Goods Shipped with IOSS Number:
- No VAT collected at import
- No customs VAT declaration needed
- Faster release from customs
- IOSS number on customs declaration

EXCLUSIONS FROM OSS:

Cannot Use OSS For:
- B2B sales (use normal VAT reverse charge)
- Sales of new vehicles
- Sales requiring installation or assembly
- Excise goods (alcohol, tobacco)
- Sales where OSS optional but not chosen
- Sales from fixed establishments in destination country

Must Register Locally If:
- Physical store in another EU country
- Goods stored in another EU country (fulfillment center)
- Fixed establishment in another EU country
- Making local sales in that country

COMMON SCENARIOS:

Scenario 1: German Online Shop Selling to EU Consumers
- Sales < €10,000/year: Can use German VAT or opt into OSS
- Sales > €10,000/year: Must use OSS and destination country rates
- No stock in other EU countries
- Files quarterly OSS return

Scenario 2: Using Amazon FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon)
- Stock stored in multiple EU countries
- Each country where stock stored = local registration needed
- OSS cannot be used (goods present locally)
- Must register for VAT in each FBA country

Scenario 3: Selling Digital Services (e.g., Software)
- All sales to EU consumers via OSS
- No €10,000 threshold for B2C services
- Must always use destination country rates
- File quarterly OSS return
- No local registrations needed

Scenario 4: Non-EU Business Selling Software to EU
- Register for non-EU OSS in Germany
- Collect VAT at customer's country rate
- File quarterly return
- Single point of contact in Germany

REGISTRATION PROCESS:

Steps to Register:
1. Access BZSt portal: www.bzst.de
2. Choose OSS type (EU, Non-EU, or IOSS)
3. Provide business information
4. Provide bank details for payments
5. Receive OSS VAT ID number
6. Confirm registration via email

Required Information:
- Business name and address
- Business structure (GmbH, sole proprietor, etc.)
- VAT identification number (if EU-established)
- Bank account in SEPA area
- Contact person details
- Activities description

Time to Process:
- Usually 2-4 weeks
- Can be faster if all information correct
- Must register before first relevant sale
- Cannot backdate registration

FILING AND PAYMENT:

Filing Process:
1. Log into BZSt OSS portal
2. Select period (quarter or month for IOSS)
3. Enter sales per country and VAT rate
4. System calculates VAT due
5. Submit return electronically
6. Make payment by due date

Payment:
- SEPA bank transfer to BZSt account
- Reference number provided in portal
- Must clear by deadline
- Late payment interest: 6% annually

Corrections:
- Can correct previous periods' returns
- Submit corrected return through portal
- Adjustments reflected in next payment
- Must explain reason for correction

NIL RETURNS:
- Must file even if no sales in period
- Submit return with zero sales
- Still required to maintain registration
- Failure to file: Penalties apply

DEREGISTRATION:

When to Deregister:
- Stopped making relevant sales
- Falling below threshold (EU OSS)
- Closing business
- Moving to local registrations

How to Deregister:
- Submit request through BZSt portal
- Provide reason and effective date
- File final return up to deregistration date
- Cannot deregister mid-quarter (must wait until quarter end)

After Deregistration:
- Must register locally in destination countries if continuing sales above threshold
- Or revert to charging home country VAT if below threshold
- Cannot re-register for OSS for defined period (restrictions apply)

PENALTIES AND COMPLIANCE:

Late Filing:
- €25 per month minimum
- Can increase based on VAT due
- Automatic application
- Repeated late filing: Increased scrutiny

Incorrect Returns:
- Voluntary correction: Usually no penalty if prompt
- If discovered by authorities: Penalties apply
- Interest on underpaid VAT: 6% annually

Loss of OSS Registration:
- Repeated non-compliance
- Persistent late filing or payment
- Failure to respond to authority requests
- Would require local registrations in all countries

PRACTICAL TIPS:

1. Record Keeping:
- Maintain detailed sales records by country
- Document evidence of customer location
- Keep VAT calculation documentation
- Use accounting software with EU VAT support

2. Currency:
- Returns and payments in EUR
- Convert other currencies using ECB rates
- Use exchange rate of last day of period
- Or monthly average rates

3. Refunds to Customers:
- Adjust in period refund processed
- Reduce sales for that country
- Can result in negative return (credit)
- Credit offset against future liabilities

4. Marketplace Sellers:
- If selling via marketplace (Amazon, eBay)
- Marketplace may handle VAT collection
- Check if marketplace uses "deemed supplier" rules
- May simplify your OSS obligations

5. Software Integration:
- Use e-commerce platforms with EU VAT support
- Automated rate application by customer location
- Automated OSS return preparation
- Examples: Shopify, WooCommerce with plugins, Lexware
